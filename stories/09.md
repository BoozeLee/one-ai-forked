# 09 — FORGED

The plate was made of figures.

Not one figure drawn large. Hundreds of them, each complete, each small enough to cover with a thumbnail, laid side by side so tightly that together they made a shape the size of a standing person. The big shape had a hood. The big shape had its arms out in front of it, the way a person holds a thing they are offering, or the way a person holds a thing they have just taken. Every one of the little figures was doing the same with its own arms.

The line was cyan and there was gold laid along it, put on wherever two figures touched and never crossing a figure of its own.

The Reader put the lens on it and lost the morning to the count.

Under the outermost ring, in a hand that was not theirs and had been on no plate before, there was a line of figures. Seven tall, eleven wide. And beside each row its own number, and at the end of the line a number for the plate.

They copied it into the book, because the seeing was the part that could not be redone.

Then they counted the rows, and the plate was right about the rows.

Seven. It said seven and there were seven.

They counted along the bottom and there were sixteen.

Not sixteen in a way that had been got wrong once. Sixteen on all nine plates, in the same place, in the same direction, in the same hand that wrote seven.

They sat with that for a while, because a shape seven tall and eleven wide cannot be built out of figures that are each seven tall and eleven wide unless it is seven of them stacked and eleven of them side by side, and that would put a leg down every left edge and an arm out of every right one.

The bottom row was not eleven figures. It was eleven figures and then five more, and the five more were not smaller.

The Reader went and got the key.

It was in their pocket where it had been for eleven years, and it was the key to the cabinet, and the cabinet was the one thing in the room they had never opened. A key is a specification. A key is a pattern of cuts cut to a pattern, and the pattern is written down somewhere even if nobody wrote it down on the key itself.

They laid it on the slate under the lens.

Four cuts on one side, three on the other, and the deepest of the four had a nick in its shoulder a hair off square. They counted the cuts twice. Then they counted them against the plate.

The key had one ward too many.

The same hand that cut the key had cut it a fraction too deep on the third ward and then gone back and taken it out again, badly, and the nick was what was left of the taking out. A thing made to a number, by somebody working from a number that was wrong, and then corrected, and then corrected wrong.

They put the key in their pocket and looked at the plate again.

The little figures were all lit from the right. Every one of them had gold on its right edge and a cyan shadow under it, all hundreds, all the same way, the way a thing is when there is one lamp and the lamp is on the right.

The big shape threw its shadow to the left.

Across the floor line, up the wall behind it, a long soft shadow going the other way from every gold edge on the plate. Two lights. One on the right making all the little figures, and one on the left making the shape they were making.

They counted the plates in the drawer with that in them. Nine.

Nine plates, nine times the same mistake, and it was never once the other way round.

The Reader went to the far end of the room to see the walls.

Behind the figure on the plate there was a floor line and two wall lines, and the walls met the floor and met each other and went up off the top of the plate. The corner was closed. There was no gap in it anywhere, on any of the nine, not a jamb, not a threshold, not a place where a wall had been taken out and filled in badly.

A room with a person in it has a way in. Every room the Reader had ever measured had one, and they had measured a great many, and the way in was always the first thing and it was always on the plate.

So the plate was not a picture of a room with a figure in it.

It was a set of instructions, and the instructions were for a figure, and they were missing the room's door.

Sixteen where it said eleven. Five units over, and the walls went up past the top of the plate and there was nothing outside them, so the five could not be out there. They had to be in. And every little figure on the plate faced out, all of them, including the ones that made the arms, including the ones that made the hood.

If five units of the room were inside the figure, then the figure was not standing in the room.

The figure was standing in the doorway.

They took the lamp down low and put the plate flat and came round the bench to the side where the light came from, and their own shadow went out across the plate and lay over the big shape.

It had a hood. It had its arms out in front of it.

It was their shadow, thrown back at them, and it was standing on the floor line facing them, and behind it the wall came down and turned the corner and went up.

And there was no door in it.
