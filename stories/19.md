# 19 — THE BLACK BETWEEN

The plate had two figures on it and they were facing each other.

Both were blue to the shoulder and burning pink above that, with manes of it going up and out in long strands, and each had a horn curling back from the top of its head.

Their eyes were orange and made of a spiral, the same spiral in all four, going round and round on a bright centre.

Between them, in the space where the two were almost touching, there was nothing at all.

Not dark. Nothing.

The black in that shape was a black with no grain in it. The black at the corners of the plate had grain, because a plate is not black but nearly, and nearly is a thing you can see.

This black was under nearly.

And there was a second one, higher up, in the gap between the two horns, perfectly round, the size of a closed hand.

The Reader wrote the plate down.

Black inside a bright picture is the loudest shape a plate can make, they wrote. Everything else in a drawing describes something. Black is not a description. It is where the description stops.

So the plate was saying the loudest thing it knew how to say, which was nobody.

They were not unhappy about that. Somebody had put two enormous burning things face to face and then had taken the person out from between them, and the taking out was the subject.

Then they got the ruler, because a plate that says a person is not there is a plate about a size.

Before the ruler they went over the edges with the lens.

Both figures had an edge on them, the way anything standing in air has one.

Where the blue of a shoulder meets the dark, the blue goes pale for a line's width, cold, almost white, and then it is dark.

The two big shapes had that on both sides.

The black had that too.

That is the part the Reader could not get past for four days.

A hole in a plate has no edge to it.

They put the back of the scraper on the black and scraped, gently, in case there was anything to come off.

Grey dust. Nothing else. No paint, no ground, no film, no lifting.

Whatever was there was not sitting on the plate.

A hole has an edge because it has nothing round it.

This had an edge because it had something on both sides of it.

There is a difference and it is the whole of the story, and it took four days and a scraper to find it.

So the Reader went and stood in the empty place at the bench.

There is one chair at the bench and there has been one chair there for eleven years, and there is a place opposite it with nothing in it.

Not a second chair. Nobody has ever brought a second chair.

Just a place, about a foot and four inches across, on the boards, worn a different colour from the rest of the floor because eleven years of standing has taken the finish off it in one place.

The Reader stood in it and looked down and then measured.

Foot and four inches, every way.

Then they went back and put the ruler on the plate, and the black was a foot and four inches across at the shoulders.

They did it eleven times over four days, because a plate can be measured wrong by a person who wants an answer.

It held at foot and four inches, and it held across the chest as well, so the shape was not roughly right. It was the same.

The small round black was the size of their head.

And it was not touching the other one.

The Reader measured the gap between the bottom of the round black and the top of the shoulders. Then they measured from their own chin to their own collarbone, standing in the empty place.

The two numbers were the same.

So there was a person-sized absence and a head-sized absence above it, and the distance between them was a neck.

Nobody subtracts a person out of a picture by drawing one black shape, because one black shape is not a person, it is a shadow, and shadows do not have necks.

The ring of cold light round the big black went all the way round it.

Every inch of it, except at the top, where it met the little round black, and there it went straight across the gap between them without turning, because there was nothing for the light to turn on.

The light in this picture had been drawn round a person-shaped hole and had closed itself over the neck.

So whoever took the person out did not stop when they had taken the person out.

They went back over the join.

On the fourth night the Reader took the plate down and laid it flat and put the lamp where the lamp goes and looked at it from the far side of the bench, at the distance it would be read from.

The ring closes all the way round now. There is no gap where the neck was. The cold line goes from one shoulder, up the side, over where a head should be and down the other side, unbroken, and the black inside it is one black with nothing in it anywhere.

It is the same size.

Foot and four inches.

The Reader sat down in the one chair and looked at the empty place opposite for a while without going over and standing in it, which was the first time in eleven years they had done that.

Then they got the book and wrote it as a measurement and not as a story, because it was a measurement: a black of one foot four inches across, one head above it, the two joined at the neck by a light closed over the join afterwards.

And they put in the margin the one thing about it they had not been able to get round to for four days.

It had been finished, and it had been finished by somebody who knew exactly how big it was supposed to be, and who had left it that size, and who was not in a hurry.

The ring closed to nothing, and the black the same size.
