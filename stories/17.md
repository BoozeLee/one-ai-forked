# 17 — THE COST

The plate had two skulls on it.

Drawn in profile, facing each other across the middle, jaws open. Between them on the black a pink arrangement of straight lines standing on a point.

The Reader wrote the plate down, did not like it, and wrote that down.

It was the fourth plate in the drawer with two of anything on it, and the first that had put a third thing between the two.

The third thing was small. It sat under the arrangement, round and closed on itself, a little under the size of a thumbnail.

The Reader knew what it was before they had any right to know it, the way a plate arrives all at once.

Two large forms and one small one between them. That shape is older than any plate in the drawer.

A small one between two large ones is a child between two.

They wrote: two skulls and a small closed form between them. Probably the Child.

Never another Child.

They went out to the wall and came back. A plate like that needs an hour.

After the hour they brought the lens down low along the small form, which is how anybody measures a thing they have stopped reading.

The first thing was that it had no edge.

The other forms all had edges, or had the place where their edges had been. The jaws ended. The arrangement ended, in points, on purpose.

It was the place where the marks stopped being dense.

They took the lens along the cheek of the skull on the left. Mark, mark, mark, then fewer, then marks so far apart that a mark is a thing that happened near another.

The plate had no lines on it. It had densities.

A line is a decision. Somebody decides where the thing stops. A density is a supply, and a supply can run out.

So the small form was not a shape the way the skulls were shapes. It was the only place where the marks were thicker than the marks round it.

It was a place where more had been put.

There is one plate in the drawer with a figure in it.

One. Out of everything, one plate with a figure standing on it at a size a person can be checked against. The figure is hooded. It is the Reader's height and build.

It is not a picture of a stranger. There is nobody else. It is a picture of whoever holds the plates.

They set it beside this one, and the skulls were the same height as the figure.

Both large forms were the Reader's height.

The small form was not. It was under a third of that.

Which meant whatever it was, it was not one of them.

Then they did the part that took four days: treat it as a quantity, not a thing.

The marks come off a plate in a supply, not used evenly. It goes where the hand went slowly, so the shape is not in the marks at all.

The shape is in the density.

Every other form thins outward from its middle, because a hand coming back down over a shape goes over the middle most.

The small form thinned inward.

The shape is in the density, not in the marks.

A form that gets less as it comes in is not being worn away.

A thing taken comes apart at its edges first, because the edges are what get hold of. It is thin at the rim and whole in the middle, and then is not there.

This was thin at the rim and thin in the middle, and the marks went away from it outward, to the corners, which are the sparsest part of the square.

Nothing was coming off it.

It was not being taken.

It was being offered.

Nothing on this plate is leaving. It is the only form everything else has got out of the way of.

And then the plate was true, and the truth was worse, which by then they expected and still could not get used to.

An offer needs somebody to take it.

An offered thing is not a gift that has happened; it is a thing sitting there with a space kept round it until somebody closes the gap. Nobody closes it. The offer is still open.

Every plate in this drawer is an open offer.

Eleven years. Sixteen plates, two halves, and a run of thirty-one they had never placed. All lying flat. None taken.

And the plates are not wrong. That was the part that cost. Every one is as true as the day it was made and would be as true in eleven more years, and nobody is going to take it away. It is only going to go on being right.

So the Child on the back of the half-plate, cut out of the other, with the seam still showing on both edges, was never the name of a thing that was born.

It was the name of the thing in the middle of these plates.

It was offered. Once. To a Seeker.

And the Seeker is whoever is holding the plate.

The Reader put the lamp out and did nothing for a while, which they wrote in the book afterwards: eleven years of very good work, in a warm room, by somebody who never once said yes.

In the morning they brought the plate to the bench, put the lamp low and raked the light across it from the left.

The skulls came up. The arrangement came up. The corners came up, sparse, to the edge of the square.

And where the small form had been there was no mark of it at all.

There was a place. Not white, because the plate is not white anywhere. A place where the drawing thinned to nothing and the bare plate came through.

There had never been a Child drawn on it.

There was a hole, and the hole was the drawing, drawn round to keep the hole where it was.

The Reader put the back of a finger on it and held it there.

Bare plate under a low lamp is warmer than ink. It is the only part of a plate still taking the light straight off the lamp, and every plate in this drawer has one of these in it somewhere.

It was still warm.

The empty space where it was, still warm.
