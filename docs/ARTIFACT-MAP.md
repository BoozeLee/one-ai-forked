# The 48-artifact map

Generated by `tools/artifact_map.py` from four files on disk. Do not hand-edit;
re-run the generator. `--check` fails if this file drifts from the corpus.

## What this is, and what it is not

It is **not** a list of 48 products. The marketplace research is unambiguous
that 48 listings is a graveyard -- 44% of Gumroad products earn exactly $0, the
strongest predictor of a zero outcome is launching without an audience, and
revenue per product peaks at 2-3 products. "Buyers pay for tools, not lists":
the three highest earners in the AI-prompt niche are a $50 Photoshop script
(11,725 sales), a $129 Notion workspace (15,093) and a $40 workflow tool
(28,687). Individual art prompts top out around $4.99.

It **is** a map of one product with eight modules, where each of the 48 comic
issues is the demo case that exercises one module hardest. That is the honest
answer to "one artifact per issue": the artifact is the same artifact, and the
issue is the test.

## The eight facets

| | facet | module | what it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | INGEST | `art_ingest.py` | folder of loose art -> manifest + derivatives |
| F2 | MEASURE | `styleprint.py` | 12 axes, z-scored, one dict per image |
| F3 | VERIFY | `validate_styleprint.py` | negative controls prove the axes move |
| F4 | SYMMETRY | `rosette.py` | deterministic n-fold reference generator |
| F5 | FRAME | `postgate.py (frame)` | aspect ratio + blankable logo band |
| F6 | TEXT | `postgate.py (typeblock, corner)` | OCR band + corner marks |
| F7 | RE-KEY | `rekey.py` | another series' refs -> that series' prompts |
| F8 | LIMITS | `KNOWN_LIMITS` | the refuted designs, shipped not hidden |

**F1 and F8 have no per-issue row, on purpose.** Ingest applies to all 48 images
equally -- there is nothing to distinguish issue 7's ingest from issue 22's --
and the known-limits payload rides inside every gate of every issue. Assigning
them rows would be inventing distinctions. The table therefore assigns six
facets across the 48 issues; these two are stated here instead.

## The assignment rule

First match wins. Every row's facet is a consequence of a stated predicate on
measured values, so any row can be re-derived or disputed by hand.

| # | predicate | facet | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `register B and bilateral >= 0.70` | MEASURE | the fork: a number recovers the signature look, and the palette half is provably unrecoverable |
| 2 | `register D` | FRAME | an inked comic page: the only reference where the frame gate's logo band is the thing under test |
| 3 | `text is an {allow:[...]} list` | TEXT | named strings are exactly what the type-block gate is about |
| 4 | `paper_frac >= 0.15` | RE-KEY | a cream plate; the one ground where paper_frac separates the corpus with a 2.4x gap (plates >= 0.237, next value down 0.113) |
| 5 | `register A or C` | SYMMETRY | plate and diagram registers: the corpus atom is a rosette at dead centre, so the model must be HANDED a correct n-fold reference |
| 6 | `otherwise` | VERIFY | the plainest images, which is where a dead axis would hide |

Thresholds are measured, not guessed: `bilateral >= 0.7` (its q75
is 0.978) and `paper_frac >= 0.15` (q75 is 0.070, the five cream plates are all
at 0.237 or above, the next value down is 0.113).

## A limit this map found in the F5 measurement

The F5 facet asks `paper_frac >= 0.15`, so it reports the ground of the two
register-D issues. One of them is below the threshold, and the reason is
worth stating because it is a limit of the axis, not a fault in the label:

| n | register D | measured `paper_frac` | clears 0.15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | inked comic page | 0.24 | yes |
| 33 | inked comic page | 0.11 | **no** |

`paper_frac` measures *visible* ground. Issue 33 is a comic page on a cream
artboard, but its panels cover the board, so the visible cream is a minority
of the frame and the axis under-counts it. The image really is on cream; the
number cannot see the part that is behind the artwork.

This is why the F5 ladder keys on the measurement rather than the register
label, and why it is reported as `paper_frac` alongside the verdict instead of
being asserted. An earlier draft of this map called the register label itself
defective; that was wrong and is withdrawn. It assumed a reference-*anchored*
pipeline in which the numbered source image is uploaded to the model. This
pipeline is self-contained -- `REGISTER_SPEC` in `tools/prompts_chatgpt.py`
supplies the medium, palette, line spec and value structure as prompt text, and
`header()` only names a corpus reference when `issues_chatgpt.json` sets
`reference`, which is `None` for issues 10, 33, 40 and 41. No reference image is
uploaded to those prompts, so a dark source image cannot contradict a cream
instruction. Registers D and E are consistent as specified.

## The honest caveat

The twelve axes measure **series membership, not family identity.**
Single-linkage clustering of all 48 in z-scored axis space puts 42 of them in one
cluster containing 9 of the 13 Dropbox sets -- purity 0.10. No cut on any axis
separates the visual families. So `rekey.py` does not detect a family and does
not cluster into one: **the buyer names the reference image per target.**
Choosing the reference is a creative act and no statistic here is entitled to
make it.

The same negative result is the sales argument. The fork's layout half is
measurable (blurred mirror correlation separates it cleanly), but its palette
half is **not** recoverable from pixels: a bilaterally mirrored fork swaps hue
across the seam by construction, so the left and right hue histograms are
near-identical by design. Three separate detectors were tried and all three
failed. **A fork cannot be obtained from a prompt, only from a reference.**
